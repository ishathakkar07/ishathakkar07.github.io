The know-how · A beginner's guide

Find the good stuff.
Skip the guesswork.

Thrifting tips for beginners, built around a student budget and a wardrobe you'll actually use. A little intention makes the search more enjoyable.

01

Give your trip a small plan.

Set a total spending limit before you browse. Write down one or two useful pieces you are looking for, such as a cardigan or a pair of trousers. Leave enough room in the budget for cleaning or a simple repair if a find needs it.

02

Shop the fit, not just the tag.

Try a piece on when you can, or bring the measurements of a similar item you already own. Check shoulder width, length, and comfort when sitting or reaching. A bargain that never fits comfortably is still money spent.

03

Take one slow lap around the garment.

Look at cuffs, collars, underarms, pockets, and inside seams. Test buttons and zippers. Check for stains, holes, pilling, and stretched fabric. Decide whether you can realistically handle any repair before you buy.

04

Picture it in your actual wardrobe.

Name two outfits you could make with the piece using clothes you already own. If you would need several other purchases to wear it, pause. You can admire something without needing to bring it home.

05

Make care part of the decision.

Read the fabric and care labels. Think about whether the piece needs special cleaning, ironing, or storage. Before wearing a secondhand garment, clean it according to its care instructions.

06

Give yourself permission to leave empty-handed.

A thrift trip can be useful even without a purchase. Notice the colors, textures, and shapes you like. Take those ideas into your next visit instead of buying something almost right just to make the trip feel successful.

A few good questions

Before your first browse.

How much should I plan to spend?

Choose an amount that works for your own budget and decide it before you enter a shop. There is no required minimum. A small list and a firm limit can help you compare a find with what you actually need.

What if I like a piece but don't know how to style it?

Start with something familiar: your usual jeans, a plain top, or shoes you wear often. Try the vintage outfit ideas for examples of building a look around a single interesting piece.

Can I visit the shops listed on Thrift IC?

No. The directory contains fictional shops, descriptions, and prices created with AI for a digital marketing assignment. You can explore the profiles as examples of a website concept, but they are not real stores or shopping destinations.
